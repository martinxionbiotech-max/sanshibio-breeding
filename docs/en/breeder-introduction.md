# Racing Pigeon Import and Quarantine Guide

> **Key takeaway**: Import quarantine is the first line of defence for any bloodline library. This guide sets out the five-step protocol — bird selection, health screening, isolation, genetic assessment and adaptation — and combines health screening ([pathogen testing](https://sanshibio.net/virus-detection/)) with pedigree and flight-ability genetic assessment, so fanciers avoid importing diseased birds or faked pedigrees and every purchase is aimed at a real goal.

Importing stock is the shortcut to upgrading a breeding programme, and also its most concentrated risk: a new breeder pigeon may carry pathogens, may have a misstated pedigree, and may simply fail to adapt. **Import quarantine** exists to defuse all three risks before the bird joins the loft — isolation blocks pathogens, testing verifies health, and genetics anchors the pedigree.

## Why imported breeders must be quarantined before they join the loft

A new breeder pigeon mixed straight into the existing flock can cause two systemic consequences:

1. **Pathogen spread**: an infected but symptom-free carrier looks normal, yet can pass Newcastle disease, pigeon circovirus, Salmonella and more to the whole flock — often vertically — ruining an entire breeding season.
2. **Pedigree breakdown**: forged certificates and misrepresented crosses turn a "premium purchase" into a "premium price for a name" and contaminate the purity of the bloodline library.

Both risks share one trait: they are invisible to the eye. That is why isolation plus testing must be used together, and that is the core of this guide.

## The five-step import workflow

| Step | Content | Core purpose |
|------|---------|--------------|
| 1. Bird selection | Define breeding goals; select by race distance and bloodline direction | Stay targeted, avoid blind purchases |
| 2. Health screening | Sample for pathogen testing plus clinical observation | Exclude carriers |
| 3. Isolation and quarantine | Isolate separately for 21–30 days of observation | Block transmission during the incubation period |
| 4. Genetic assessment | Flight ability plus pedigree (paternity / inbreeding coefficient) testing | Verify pedigree and genetic potential |
| 5. Adaptation management | Gradual transition of feed, environment and training | Join the loft smoothly and perform |

The five steps interlock: **screening runs in parallel with isolation, assessment links up with adaptation**. Cutting corners at any step discounts every investment that follows.

## How it works: the molecular logic behind health screening and genetic assessment

**Health screening**: the laboratory uses **qPCR (real-time quantitative PCR)** to detect pathogen nucleic acid (DNA/RNA) directly, with high sensitivity and specificity, so silent carriers can be found before symptoms appear. This matters especially for breeder pigeons, which are the main drivers of vertical transmission. Pathogen characteristics are catalogued in the [Pathogen Encyclopedia](https://pathogens.sanshibio.net/).

**Genetic assessment**: pedigree verification relies on typing **SNP (single nucleotide polymorphism)** loci. Sanshi Bio uses 58 SNP loci for [paternity testing](paternity-test.md) against suspected parents and estimates the [inbreeding coefficient](inbreeding-coefficient.md) from genome-wide homozygosity; flight ability is assessed through eight functional genes (LDHA endurance, MSTN muscle power, DRD4 homing, CRY1 navigation, F-KER feather structure, LRP8 learning and memory, GSR orientation in rain, CASK cognition) — see [flight-ability gene testing](https://sanshibio.net/flight-ability/). DNA never changes, so a single feather sample already reveals the hand at the moment of purchase.

## Isolation and quarantine in practice

1. **Separate cage, separate loft**: keep new arrivals strictly apart from the rest of the flock, with dedicated staff and equipment, to avoid cross-contamination.
2. **Observation records**: log feed intake, water intake, droppings, demeanour and respiratory state daily; flag anything abnormal immediately.
3. **Minimum duration**: isolate for **21–30 days** (covering the incubation period of most pathogens), with no contact with other pigeons during that time.
4. **Sample in parallel**: pull 4–6 feathers with intact follicles at the start of isolation and send them for pathogen and genetic testing, so you are waiting on reports while the bird is still in isolation.
5. **Ending isolation**: only when pathogen tests are negative and no abnormalities have been observed may the bird join the loft and be paired.

> 💡 Tip: isolation is not "a few days in a cage", it is a complete cycle of observation, testing and assessment. The isolation period is the right time to complete genetic assessment and prepare for your [pairing strategy](pairing-strategy.md).

## Health screening: the pathogen checklist for incoming breeders

| Pathogen group | Representative pathogens | Why screen at import |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| Viruses | Newcastle disease virus, avian influenza virus, pigeon circovirus, pigeon adenovirus, pigeon pox / herpesvirus | Highly contagious and prone to outbreaks; high vertical-transmission risk |
| Bacteria | Salmonella (paratyphoid), Mycoplasma, Chlamydia psittaci | Silent carriage; affects reproduction and squab survival |
| Parasites / fungi | Trichomonas, Candida | Affects crop and digestive-tract health |

Health screening is a precondition for import, not an optional extra. Birds intended for the core breeding flock in particular must be tested before they enter.

## Genetic assessment: pedigree verification plus flight ability

Beyond health, an import must have its identity and its potential verified:

1. **[Paternity testing](paternity-test.md)**: confirms who a bird's parents are, giving the pedigree certificate DNA backing and ruling out misrepresentation.
2. **[Inbreeding coefficient testing](inbreeding-coefficient.md)**: measures how closely bred the incoming bird is, giving a basis for avoiding close matings later.
3. **[Flight-ability gene testing](https://sanshibio.net/flight-ability/)**: quantifies genetic potential for endurance, speed, homing and navigation, so you can judge whether the bird is worth the price and which distances suit it.

All three tests share one feather sample and one SNP panel, so they can be run together. For the mechanisms behind the flight genes, see the [flight gene knowledge base](https://genes.sanshibio.net/).

## Managing the adaptation period

1. **Feed transition**: replace old feed with new gradually in fixed proportions over 7–10 days to reduce stress.
2. **Water and environment**: keep drinking water clean and avoid frequent loft changes or hard training early on.
3. **Condition checks**: monitor body weight, feather quality and demeanour recovery to judge whether the bird is ready for breeding or training.
4. **Record and integrate**: file test reports, pedigree records and health notes together under [pedigree records management](pedigree-management.md).

## Key points

1. Quarantine before integrating — 21–30 days of isolation plus pathogen testing is the first barrier against infection.
2. Health screening runs on molecular testing — qPCR detects pathogen nucleic acid directly and exposes silent carriers.
3. Pedigree is verified by DNA — paternity testing plus the inbreeding coefficient give the certificate substance.
4. Potential is quantified by genes — the eight flight-ability genes let you assess genetic value before you buy.
5. The adaptation period is not optional — transition feed and environment gradually so the new breeder settles in and performs.

## FAQ

### How long should a newly purchased breeder pigeon be isolated?

Isolate for **21–30 days** (3–4 weeks) to cover the incubation period of most pathogens. Complete pathogen testing and genetic assessment during isolation, and release the bird into the loft only when results are negative and no abnormalities are observed.

### Which tests are essential when importing a breeder pigeon?

At least two groups: **health screening** ([pathogen testing](https://sanshibio.net/virus-detection/) to rule out carriers) and **genetic assessment of pedigree and potential** ([paternity testing](paternity-test.md), [inbreeding coefficient testing](inbreeding-coefficient.md), [flight-ability gene testing](https://sanshibio.net/flight-ability/)). Health and pedigree verified together is what makes an import sound.

### Should imported breeder pigeons be paternity tested?

Strongly recommended. Pedigree certificates can be altered or forged, and [paternity testing](paternity-test.md) uses DNA evidence to confirm who a bird's parents are — the most effective defence against paying a premium for a stolen identity.

### Do apparently healthy breeder pigeons still need pathogen testing?

Yes. Many pathogens are carried silently: a bird looks completely normal yet can pass infection to its offspring vertically. qPCR molecular testing detects carriers during the asymptomatic stage and is an irreplaceable part of health screening.

### How soon after import can a breeder pigeon be paired?

Wait until quarantine (21–30 days) is complete, pathogen tests are negative, and [inbreeding coefficient testing](inbreeding-coefficient.md) and a health assessment are done; then pair according to your [pairing strategy](pairing-strategy.md), avoiding pairing while infected or breeding blindly close.

### Does feather sampling for testing harm the pigeon?

No. Pulling 4–6 breast or belly feathers with intact follicles yields enough DNA — no blood draw is needed, the impact on the bird is minimal, and samples can be taken and sent on the day of purchase.
