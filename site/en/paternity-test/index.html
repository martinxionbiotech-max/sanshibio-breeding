    Racing Pigeon Paternity Testing Guide: Principles, Workflow and Pedigree Certification¶

    Key takeaway: Racing pigeon paternity testing confirms a bird's parentage through DNA comparison and is a core tool for pedigree certification and breeding management. Built on Mendel's laws of inheritance, it uses multiple SNP loci to calculate a paternity index and combined paternity probability, with exclusion accuracy above 99.9% — far beyond traditional reliance on rings and paper certificates.

    In racing pigeon trade and breeding, pedigree is central to value. Traditional methods based on rings and certificates, however, cannot rule out forgery or record mix-ups. Paternity testing replaces subjective judgement with genetic evidence, making a pedigree genuinely verifiable and traceable.

    The science behind racing pigeon paternity testing¶

    Paternity testing rests on Mendel's laws of inheritance — half of a squab's genes come from the sire and half from the dam. The laboratory selects multiple highly polymorphic SNP loci and compares the alleles of the offspring with those of the alleged parents, judging by two lines of reasoning:

    1. Exclusion: if the alleged parent lacks an allele the offspring must have inherited, the relationship is excluded outright
    2. Confirmation: if the genotype fully follows the rules of inheritance, the paternity index (PI) and combined paternity probability are calculated and the relationship confirmed once the threshold is reached

    Sanshi Bio uses 58 proprietary SNP loci (57 SNP loci plus a sex locus). More loci and higher polymorphism mean precise, reliable results.

    How it works: SNP loci and the paternity index¶

    At its core, racing pigeon paternity testing compares polymorphic genetic markers, of which two types are common:

    Marker type Full name Characteristics Typical use
    STR Short tandem repeat (microsatellite) Highly polymorphic; traditional in forensic work Human relationship testing
    SNP Single nucleotide polymorphism Abundant, stable, easily standardised, high throughput High-throughput individual identification and parentage determination

    A paternity index (PI) is calculated as the ratio of the probability of observing the current genotype under the hypothesis that the alleged parent is the true parent, against the hypothesis that a random individual is the parent. The larger the PI, the stronger the evidence for the relationship. Multiplying the PI values of many loci gives the combined paternity index (CPI), which is converted into a combined paternity probability — 99.99% or above confirms the relationship, while a locus incompatible with Mendelian inheritance supports an exclusion conclusion.

    For why SNP loci identify individuals so precisely, see the genetics basics for racing pigeons.

    Where paternity testing is used¶

    Scenario Value
    Pedigree certification Verify the pedigree of a noted bird and rule out forged certificates
    Breeding management Confirm the outcome of pairings and prevent mismatched lines
    Race eligibility checks Verify the pedigree of entries and protect fair competition
    Import traceability Confirm the lineage of imported breeders
    Pedigree building Build a reliable pedigree archive for a new family

    The testing workflow¶

    1. Enquiry and order: define the subjects (offspring plus alleged sire/dam) and the test required
    2. Feather sampling: pull 4–6 breast or belly feathers from the offspring and each alleged parent (with the root follicle intact)
    3. Laboratory testing: extract DNA and complete the parentage comparison across 58 SNP loci
    4. Reporting: an authentication conclusion with the combined paternity probability, used as the basis for pedigree certification

    For the full workflow see the racing pigeon paternity testing service page. For sampling standards see the gene bank sampling and submission workflow, or contact the Sanshi Bio technical team.

    💡 Tip: when pairing, record the pedigree and retain samples at the same time, so lineage can be traced later. A one-parent test (with only one parent supplied) is possible, but a both-parent test gives a stronger confirmation.

    Relationship to gene ID cards and AB-pigeon testing¶

    Together the three form a complete system for pigeon identity and pedigree management:

    • Gene ID card: builds a unique genetic profile for each racing pigeon (58 SNP loci by next-generation sequencing)
    • AB-pigeon testing: identifies same-name substitute birds and prevents fraud
    • Paternity testing: confirms the parent–offspring relationship between birds

    Combined with sex identification and inbreeding coefficient testing, this gives a complete pedigree archive covering identity, lineage, inbreeding and sex — see the home page of this knowledge base.

    Key points¶

    1. Paternity testing rests on parentage comparison — comparing SNP loci to determine the parent–offspring relationship.
    2. Exclusion accuracy is high — molecular testing excludes a non-parent with 99.9% or greater accuracy.
    3. Feathers are enough — no blood draw, and testing is possible from the squab stage.
    4. Three main applications — pedigree certification, breeding management and race eligibility checks.
    5. Complementary to the gene ID card — profiling plus relationship testing manage a pedigree together.

    FAQ¶

    What samples does paternity testing need?¶

    Pulling 4–6 breast or belly feathers with the root follicle intact is enough. No blood draw is required, the impact on the pigeon is minimal, and samples can be taken as early as the squab stage.

    Does paternity testing need samples from both parents?¶

    Confirmation is stronger when both parents are sampled; a single parent also supports a one-parent test, though locus coverage needs to be assessed. See the racing pigeon paternity testing service workflow for details.

    How accurate is paternity testing?¶

    Based on SNP locus parentage comparison, the accuracy of excluding a non-parent reaches 99.9% or higher, making it a reliable method for determining relationships.

    What is the difference between paternity testing and a gene ID card?¶

    A gene ID card answers "which pigeon is this" and builds a unique identity profile for one bird; paternity testing answers "who are this pigeon's parents" and confirms the relationship between birds. Together they give complete pedigree traceability.

    Can squabs be paternity tested?¶

    Yes. Feathers yield DNA, so testing is possible at the squab stage, which helps confirm the pedigree early and keep breeding records in order.

    Can I run a paternity test with only one known parent?¶

    Yes. A single parent sample supports a one-parent test, but the strength of the conclusion depends on locus coverage, so supplying both parents is recommended where possible.

    How long does a paternity test take to report?¶

    The automated sequencing workflow is efficient; the exact turnaround is set out in the testing service description.
