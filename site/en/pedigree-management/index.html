    Racing Pigeon Pedigree Management Guide¶

    Key takeaway: Racing pigeon pedigree management is essentially an upgrade from "a bloodline recorded on paper" to "a bloodline anchored in DNA". Through three steps — building a gene ID card, tracing parentage by paternity testing and controlling the inbreeding coefficient — plus digital lineage records, fanciers can build a pedigree archive that cannot be tampered with and can be traced generation after generation, cutting pedigree fraud off at the root.

    A famous racing pigeon's value is largely underwritten by its "bloodline". Yet in real trading and breeding, forged pedigree certificates, muddled records and confusion between birds sharing a name are common — a bloodline on paper can be altered, whereas the bloodline in DNA cannot be faked. Racing pigeon pedigree management exists to resolve exactly this contradiction: use genetic technology to "hard-code" the bloodline into DNA, then use digital tools to keep it managed.

    Why traditional pedigree records lose their accuracy¶

    Traditional pedigree records rely on ring numbers and paper certificates, and they carry three systemic weaknesses:

    Weakness How it shows up Consequence
    Alterable Certificates can be forged, numbers changed and rings swapped Inflated lineage for famous birds; trade fraud
    Confusable Birds with the same name or the same ring number are hard to tell apart "AB-bird" substitution in races
    Unverifiable No objective means of verification — records are purely subjective Errors cannot be traced

    The root of these weaknesses is that traditional records capture "symbols", not "genes". DNA, by contrast, never changes and cannot be forged — a natural anchor for a pedigree archive.

    Molecular mechanism: how DNA becomes a record that never fades¶

    The genetic anchor of a pedigree archive rests on two classes of molecular marker:

    • SNP (single nucleotide polymorphism): a variation at a single base position in the genome. Sanshi Bio uses 58 highly polymorphic SNP loci; the number of possible combinations is enormous, so the probability that any two pigeons match completely is vanishingly small — enough to form a unique "genetic fingerprint".
    • STR (short tandem repeat): a difference in the number of repeats of a DNA sequence. Highly polymorphic, it is the classic marker of traditional parentage testing.

    Each has its own emphasis: SNPs are numerous and stable to genotype, well suited to high-throughput standardisation, and are the workhorse of pedigree archives and individual identification; STRs are highly polymorphic and often complement parentage determination. A genotype never changes, so one profile is valid for life — the molecular basis of a bloodline record that never fades.

    The three pillars of a racing pigeon pedigree archive¶

    A complete racing pigeon pedigree archive should contain three mutually supporting layers:

    1. Identity layer (gene ID card): build a unique genetic identity for every pigeon and answer "who is this bird?". See Gene ID card.
    2. Parentage layer (paternity testing): confirm parent–offspring relationships and answer "who are this bird's parents?". The subsite entry is paternity testing, and the service page is racing pigeon paternity testing.
    3. Bloodline quality layer (inbreeding coefficient): quantify how inbred the bloodline is, informing pairing decisions and preventing inbreeding depression. See inbreeding coefficient testing.

    Stacked together, the three layers reconstruct a pigeon's full pedigree and put DNA evidence behind every relationship.

    Practical steps: the four-step method for building a loft pedigree archive¶

    1. Take stock of what to archive: list breeder pigeons, key racers and core squabs, and set your priorities.
    2. Sample and retain: pluck 4–6 feathers with intact follicles from the chest and abdomen of each bird, place them in individual sample bags labelled with the ring number, and record sex identification at the same time.
    3. Submit for testing and file the results: send samples to the Sanshi Bio laboratory, complete 58-SNP genotyping, and collect the gene ID card and paternity test report.
    4. Digitise the lineage: enter genotypes, relationships, race results and health records into a spreadsheet or pedigree software to form a searchable, traceable digital archive, and back it up regularly.

    💡 Tip: a pedigree archive is not a one-off job you finish and forget. We recommend a concentrated update of offspring records at the end of every breeding season so the lineage rolls forward.

    Key points¶

    1. The anchor of a pedigree archive is DNA — the genotype never changes and cannot be forged, far beyond what a paper certificate can offer.
    2. Three pillars: the gene ID card fixes identity, paternity testing fixes parentage, and the inbreeding coefficient fixes bloodline quality.
    3. Build once, valid for life — a feather sample is enough, and squabs can be archived too.
    4. Digital lineage records are the key — only by integrating genetic evidence with race and health data does an archive deliver value over the long term.
    5. Filing is anti-fraud — a complete archive is the trusted basis for trade and race verification.

    FAQ¶

    What is the difference between a pedigree certificate and a gene ID card?¶

    A pedigree certificate records lineage and race results — a "symbolic record" that can be altered or faked; a gene ID card is an identity record at the DNA level — a "genetic record" that cannot be forged or substituted. Only together do they make a bloodline traceable and trustworthy. See Gene ID card.

    How often does a pedigree archive need updating?¶

    A pigeon's genotype never changes, so a gene identity profile built once remains valid for life. Pedigree data, however, needs continuous updating — we recommend adding records and paternity tests for each new generation after every breeding season, so the lineage rolls forward.

    Can a pigeon without a pedigree certificate still have an archive?¶

    Yes. A pedigree archive does not require ancestors' certificates as a precondition. As long as you have a feather sample from the bird, you can build a gene ID card and retain the genotype first; parentage relationships can then be filled in step by step through paternity testing to reconstruct the pedigree.

    How long can a feather sample be stored?¶

    Feathers with an intact follicle keep for several months under dry, dark, room-temperature conditions — long enough to complete testing. For long-term storage, keep them cold and dry as the original evidence for future re-testing and traceability.

    Can a pedigree archive prevent pedigree fraud?¶

    Yes. A pedigree archive is anchored in DNA, so any substitution, cross-mating or impersonation will be exposed in a genetic comparison. Combined with pre-race verification under race anti-fraud, it squeezes the room for fraud at the root.

    Does sampling for a pedigree archive hurt the pigeon?¶

    No. DNA can be extracted from 4–6 feathers plucked from the chest and abdomen — no blood draw is needed, the impact is minimal, and squabs can be sampled safely too.
